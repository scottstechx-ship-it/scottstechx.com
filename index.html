Target architecture · governed delivery

Sentinel AI
Security Ecosystem

A reference architecture for phased delivery of authorized offensive assurance, defensive operations, AI-assisted response, and executive oversight.

Important status

This page describes a Reference architecture for phased delivery. It does not represent a currently deployed SOC platform or autonomous production capability.

Operating model

One governed control plane

Zero Trust identity, immutable evidence, policy enforcement, and Human approval bind every security workflow together.

Never trust, always verifyStrong identity, device posture, least privilege, and continuous authorization.
Safe autonomyBounded playbooks, policy checks, dry runs, and reversible actions before execution.
Evidence by defaultSigned audit events and chain-of-custody records for operational and compliance review.
Capability domains

Designed for control, not unchecked autonomy

Each domain is independently deployable. Integration proceeds only after identity, telemetry quality, rollback, and audit controls pass acceptance testing.

01 · Authorized assurance

Offensive validation with hard boundaries

A signed Rules of Engagement engine constrains every action by target, technique, operator, environment, and time window. Out-of-scope work is blocked and recorded.

  • • Passive-first discovery and CMDB reconciliation
  • • Safe vulnerability validation without disruptive payloads
  • • MITRE ATT&CK-aligned simulations with kill switches
  • • Signed findings, evidence, and remediation workflow

02 · Defensive operations

Unified detection and response

Normalize network, endpoint, identity, cloud, application, and data telemetry into evidence-backed detections with transparent confidence and provenance.

  • • SIEM, EDR/XDR, NTA, IAM, DLP, CSPM integration
  • • Detection-as-code with peer review and rollback
  • • Threat hunting and entity behavior baselines
  • • Case management with chain-of-custody

03 · AI coordination

Explainable assistance before automation

Specialized agents enrich events, retrieve approved runbooks, simulate response options, and provide evidence citations. Human approval remains mandatory for disruptive actions.

  • • Recon, Monitor, Response, and Compliance agents
  • • Policy-bound tools with minimum necessary privilege
  • • Evaluation gates for drift, hallucination, and bias
  • • Full prompt, context, tool, and decision auditing

04 · Governance

Measurable risk and compliance evidence

Map assets, controls, risks, findings, incidents, and evidence to NIST CSF, ISO 27001, OWASP ASVS, MITRE ATT&CK, and applicable regulatory obligations.

  • • Versioned policies and control ownership
  • • Continuous evidence collection and attestation
  • • Executive risk trends without invented certainty
  • • Audit-ready export with access logging
Non-negotiable guardrails

Authority before capability

Security tooling is powerful. Sentinel AI is designed to fail closed when authorization, scope, identity, or evidence controls are missing.

Signed Rules of Engagement

Cryptographically signed, time-bound, target-bound authorization is verified at dispatch and execution.

Human approval gates

Account disablement, isolation, traffic blocking, data changes, and active testing require accountable approval unless a specific reversible playbook is pre-authorized.

Blast-radius controls

Concurrency limits, canaries, maintenance windows, kill switches, rollback, and asset criticality checks constrain execution.

Tamper-evident evidence

Commands, approvals, model versions, retrieved sources, outputs, and results are signed and retained under policy.

Delivery roadmap

Prove the controls before scaling the autonomy

Read technical blueprint

  1. Phase 1 · Foundation

    Identity, asset graph, event contracts, audit, command center shell

    Exit only after threat modeling, access reviews, restore tests, and telemetry quality gates pass.

  2. Phase 2 · Defense

    SIEM ingestion, detection-as-code, cases, cloud and identity posture

    Measure coverage, latency, precision, analyst workload, and recovery—not marketing guarantees.

  3. Phase 3 · Assurance

    Rules of Engagement, safe validation, attack-path analysis, remediation

    Begin in a lab, then approved non-production targets, then bounded production validation.

  4. Phase 4 · Assisted response

    RAG, investigation copilots, simulations, pre-approved reversible playbooks

    Production promotion requires model evaluation, red teaming, drift monitoring, and manual fallback.

  5. Phase 5 · Resilience

    Multi-region recovery, chaos tests, evidence automation, continuous purple teaming

    Scale against measured workloads and agreed SLOs; revisit the threat model every quarter.
